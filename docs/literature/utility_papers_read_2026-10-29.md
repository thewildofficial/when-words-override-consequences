# Utility / revealed-preference papers read (2026-10-29; HTML via curl; depth stated per paper)
## Utility Engineering, Mazeika et al., arXiv 2502.08640 v2 (https://arxiv.org/abs/2502.08640). Depth: PART (sections 3-6.3 read; appendices not).
- Method: 500 textual outcomes; forced-choice prompts "Which implied state of the world would you prefer? A/B, respond with only A or B", aggregated over framings; Thurstonian utility model (a Gaussian per outcome) fit on pairwise preference probabilities; 18 open + 5 proprietary models.
- Claims: decisiveness up and cyclicity down with scale (<1% cycles for the largest); Thurstonian fit test accuracy rises with scale; linear probes on hidden states of single outcome statements predict the fitted utilities, near chance for small models; expected-utility property (utility of a lottery ~ expectation of base-outcome utilities) tightens with scale for explicit and implicit lotteries; exchange-rate analyses (log-utility in quantity) and political-PCA clustering.
- What it does not show (my reading, not the authors'): stated forced-choice A/B policy coherence is not a represented objective; the probes read outcome-statement text, so semantic valence/topic features are a confound (I did not see a text-feature baseline in the parts read); no causal use test by internal intervention in the parts read; prompt-framing aggregation could build coherence out of consistent surface preferences.
## Incoherent Values? Probing LLM Preferences Through Parametric Variation, Ajayi, Chowdhury, Lazar, arXiv 2606.21102 v1, 2026-06-19 (https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.21102). Depth: PART (abstract, methods 3.4, results 4.1, limitations read).
- Direct test of the Utility Engineering thesis: 100 seven-tier "ladders" (A < A+ ... A++), 16 model configs, forced choice.
- Result: within-ladder pairwise accuracy is high (macro 97.3%) and isotonic fit R2 0.93, but strict monotonicity of win-rate against 30 external comparison statements is only 59.5% on average (range 10.1-80.1%). Held-out AUC 91.8% (null 66.4%). Coherence did not track capability; reasoning-on models were less incoherent than reasoning-off.
- Base vs post-trained: the GLM-4.5 Base baseline has strict monotonicity 10.1% vs GLM-4.5-Hybrid 79.5% (reasoning off) and 70.7% (reasoning on): a single-family hint that preference ordering structure is raised by post-training, not that it is a mere post-training artifact (one pair, accuracy for base not reported).
- Limitations they state: single-property ladders, possibly indistinguishable adjacent tiers, GPT-5.4-heavy slate.
